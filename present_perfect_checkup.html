The Present Perfect

Work through the steps on your own. Each step has a quick check. At the end you can practise and win three stars.

Step 1

What is it about?

Merksatz

Present perfect wird gewählt,
wenn einzig das Ergebnis zählt
oder wenn’s bis heute hält.

Read Maya’s message to her old friend Chloe. Look at the blue words.

Hi Chloe! We have moved to Glasgow. I have already unpacked my boxes, but my brother hasn’t found his football yet. Dad’s car has broken down again, so we can’t go to the beach. Have you ever been to Scotland? Come and visit me! Love, Maya

All the blue words are in the present perfect. Something happened in the past, but Maya is interested in now: her boxes are empty now, the car doesn’t work now.

Quick check: Why does Maya say “I have unpacked my boxes”?

Step 3

When do you use it?

1. Result now

Something happened. You can see the result now.

I have lost my phone. (I can’t call you now.)

2. Experiences

Things you have done in your life (or never done) up to now.

Have you ever been to London? – No, I have never been there.

3. Just, already, yet

Something happened a short time ago, or not up to now.

The bus has just left. Have you finished yet?

4. Since and for

Something started in the past and is still going on now.

We have lived in Schwerin since 2020 / for six years.

Quick check: “I’ve broken my arm, so I can’t play football.” Which use is it?

Step 5

Present perfect or simple past?

Do you say when it happened, with a finished time? Then use the simple past.

yesterdaylast weektwo days agoin 2019when I was six

Present perfect

No time given. The result or experience matters now.

I have lost my key.

Simple past

A finished time in the past.

I lost my key yesterday.

Careful: Never use the present perfect with yesterday, last …, … ago or in 2019. “I have lost my key yesterday” is wrong.
